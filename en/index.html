About

About me

M.S. in Port, Coastal and Offshore Engineering at Hohai University, researching navigation attitude sensing, multimodal typhoon data fusion and large-model inference. At China's Ministry of Water Resources I worked on localizing the US POSSIM storm-surge mapping model for Chinese waters — building an automatic parsing and fusion pipeline for heterogeneous CMA and JTWC typhoon data, and re-calibrating key parameters (radius of maximum wind, central pressure) together with warning thresholds.

Using AI weather models I forecast typhoon tracks and intensity with 1/3/6/24-hour rolling inference plus XGBoost-based intensity correction, then couple the corrected wind and pressure fields into the ADCIRC+SWAN model for storm surge and wave simulation. I am a proficient user of SMS for meshing and pre-processing, and of ArcGIS / QGIS for mapping and analysis. I hold 1 granted and 4 pending invention patents, 1 software copyright and 2 EI-indexed papers; in 2025 I received both the Best Student Paper Award and the Best Student Presentation Award at APAC 2025 (the only recipients from China), along with the Huiyan Education & Technology Fund Scholarship.

Projects

Selected projects

Large model · Numerical modeling Mar 2024 – May 2026

AI Weather Model Driven Typhoon Tracking and Storm Surge Simulation

Typhoon track and intensity forecasting with AI weather models, using 1/3/6/24-hour rolling inference and XGBoost-based dynamic intensity correction. The corrected wind and pressure fields are reconstructed and coupled into the ADCIRC+SWAN model, closing the chain from meteorological forcing to nearshore surge and wave prediction. The best 1-hour scheme cut central-pressure MAE by 46–52% and maximum-wind MAE by 51–65%; the best surge scheme reached a total water level RMSE of 0.17 m, a mean maximum-surge error of 0.13 m and a peak-timing error of 0.9 h — 1–1.7 h shorter than the operational JTWC scheme.
